# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site is for hospitals, governments, CSR teams, accessibility organizations, institutions, and end users who need to understand Kaiteki's accessibility work, evaluate credibility, and decide whether to partner, deploy, fund, or advocate for its solutions.

Visitors may arrive with different levels of context: procurement and CSR teams looking for evidence, healthcare and institutional leaders looking for practical deployment fit, accessibility organizations looking for alignment, and end users looking for dignity-first solutions that speak to lived needs.

## Product Purpose

The site exists to tell the story of inclusive infrastructure with clarity and evidence. It should explain the problem Kaiteki addresses, show the impact of its work, build confidence in the GUKO product and future infrastructure roadmap, and move qualified partners toward a conversation.

Success looks like a visitor leaving with three things: trust in the team, belief in the impact, and a clear next step to collaborate.

## Brand Personality

Human, trustworthy, impactful.

The voice should feel grounded and specific. It should lead with dignity, real-world accessibility, practical deployment, and institutional credibility. It should avoid hype, technical posturing, and generic startup language.

## Anti-references

This should not look like a SaaS dashboard, an AI startup landing page, or a corporate consulting website.

Avoid software-style metric panels, generic gradient technology visuals, abstract AI graphics, cold enterprise templates, overused consulting language, and layouts that make accessibility feel like a secondary service offering. The site should not feel like it is selling a platform. It should feel like it is presenting a serious social-impact infrastructure company with real deployments.

## Design Principles

1. Practice the mission visually: the interface must be legible, navigable, inclusive, and respectful from the first screen.
2. Evidence before persuasion: impact metrics, deployments, recognitions, and partnerships should carry the credibility story.
3. Dignity over drama: communicate the human stakes without pity, exaggeration, or spectacle.
4. Built for institutions and people: balance public-sector credibility with warmth for end users and caregivers.
5. Specific beats generic: use concrete product, deployment, and infrastructure language instead of broad innovation claims.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Commit to WCAG 2.2 AA. The site should use high contrast, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support, clear focus states, readable typography, semantic HTML, and descriptive alt text for all meaningful images.

Accessibility is not only a compliance layer for this project. It is a brand requirement and a proof point for the company's purpose.
